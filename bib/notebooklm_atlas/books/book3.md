**Statement / Body**

Given symbolic membranes $M_i$ and $M_j$ (Def. definition:bk3_symbolic_membrane), a coupling map $Phi_{ij}: M_i times M_j rightarrow S$ is a smooth function to a shared symbolic substrate $S$ (typically a vector space or manifold) satisfying:


- Symmetry: $Phi_{ij}(x,y) = Phi_{ji}(y,x)$ for all $x in M_i, y in M_j$.

- Boundedness: $\|Phi_{ij}(x,y)\|_S leq C_{ij}$ for some constant $C_{ij} > 0$ and an appropriate norm $\|cdot\|_S$ on $S$.

- Sensitivity: The gradients $nabla_xPhi_{ij}$ and $nabla_yPhi_{ij}$ exist and are non-vanishing on open dense subsets of $M_i$ and $M_j$ respectively.

**Verbatim LaTeX Body**

```latex
\begin{definition}[Coupling Map] \label{definition:bk3_coupling_map}
Given symbolic membranes $\mathcal{M}_i$ and $\mathcal{M}_j$ (Def.~\ref{definition:bk3_symbolic_membrane}), a coupling map $\Phi_{ij}: \mathcal{M}_i \times \mathcal{M}_j \rightarrow S$ is a smooth function to a shared symbolic substrate $S$ (typically a vector space or manifold) satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Symmetry: $\Phi_{ij}(x,y) = \Phi_{ji}(y,x)$ for all $x \in \mathcal{M}_i, y \in \mathcal{M}_j$.
    \item Boundedness: $\|\Phi_{ij}(x,y)\|_S \leq C_{ij}$ for some constant $C_{ij} > 0$ and an appropriate norm $\|\cdot\|_S$ on $S$.
    \item Sensitivity: The gradients $\nabla_x\Phi_{ij}$ and $\nabla_y\Phi_{ij}$ exist and are non-vanishing on open dense subsets of $\mathcal{M}_i$ and $\mathcal{M}_j$ respectively.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
```

vanishes when $H^1_{dR}(M)=0$. In dimension three, after
using the metric and orientation to identify forms and vector fields, the
coexact term $deltabeta$ is the conventional curl component.

For a finite-dimensional inner-product model with a chosen integrative
subspace $G$, the corresponding certified kernel is the orthogonal split

V_r=P_GV_r+(I-P_G)V_r,

whose two components are orthogonal and unique relative to $G$.

**Statement / Body**

The conceptual bridge sequence (Def. definition:bk3_conceptual_bridge_sequence)
maps $M to sigma to Sigma to N to M_{text{meta}}$.
We show the last step closes the loop.

textbf{Existence of $M_{text{meta}}$ within $M$.}
The symbolic manifold $M$ (Def. definition:bk1_symbolic_manifold) is the
space of all symbolic structures on the observer's domain. The network
$N$ (Def. definition:bk3_symbolic_network), being a finite graph
of compressed relational structures with a stability functional
$S(N) in mathbb{R}_+$, is itself a symbolic structure and
therefore an element of $M$. By Def. definition:bk3_symbolic_membrane,
any sufficiently coherent sub-region of $M$ with a well-defined boundary and
drift field qualifies as a symbolic membrane; $N$ and its dynamics
satisfy these conditions, constituting $M_{text{meta}} subset M$.

textbf{Feedback into ${M_i}$.}
Since $M_{text{meta}} subset M$ and the $M_i subset M$,
the coupling map construction (Def. definition:bk3_coupling_map) applies
between $M_{text{meta}}$ and each $M_i$. The state of
$M_{text{meta}}$ can therefore modulate the coupling strengths
$lambda_{ij}$ (Def. definition:bk3_induced_coupling_energy) and response
parameters $eta_i$ (Thm. theorem:bk3_couplinginduced_drift_modification)
of the lower-level membranes, closing the loop
$M_{text{meta}} to {M_i}$.

The composition of this feedback with the forward sequence
${M_i} to M_{text{meta}}$ is therefore a well-defined
endomorphism of the symbolic manifold $M$, establishing the closed loop.

```latex
maps $\mathcal{M} \to \sigma \to \Sigma \to \mathcal{N} \to \mathcal{M}_{\text{meta}}$.
We show the last step closes the loop.

\textbf{Existence of $\mathcal{M}_{\text{meta}}$ within $M$.}
The symbolic manifold $M$ (Def.~\ref{definition:bk1_symbolic_manifold}) is the
space of all symbolic structures on the observer's domain. The network
$\mathcal{N}$ (Def.~\ref{definition:bk3_symbolic_network}), being a finite graph
of compressed relational structures with a stability functional
$\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N}) \in \mathbb{R}_+$, is itself a symbolic structure and
therefore an element of $M$. By Def.~\ref{definition:bk3_symbolic_membrane},
any sufficiently coherent sub-region of $M$ with a well-defined boundary and
drift field qualifies as a symbolic membrane; $\mathcal{N}$ and its dynamics
satisfy these conditions, constituting $\mathcal{M}_{\text{meta}} \subset M$.

\textbf{Feedback into $\{\mathcal{M}_i\}$.}
Since $\mathcal{M}_{\text{meta}} \subset M$ and the $\mathcal{M}_i \subset M$,
the coupling map construction (Def.~\ref{definition:bk3_coupling_map}) applies
between $\mathcal{M}_{\text{meta}}$ and each $\mathcal{M}_i$. The state of
$\mathcal{M}_{\text{meta}}$ can therefore modulate the coupling strengths

parameters $\eta_i$ (Thm.~\ref{theorem:bk3_couplinginduced_drift_modification})

$\mathcal{M}_{\text{meta}} \to \{\mathcal{M}_i\}$.

The composition of this feedback with the forward sequence
$\{\mathcal{M}_i\} \to \mathcal{M}_{\text{meta}}$ is therefore a well-defined
endomorphism of the symbolic manifold $M$, establishing the closed loop.
\end{proof}
```

Compensatory response.
By Thm. theorem:bk3_couplinginduced_drift_modification, the coupling mechanism
produces compensatory drift adjustments $Delta D_i$ that oppose deviations from the
symbiotic equilibrium (Def. definition:bk3_symbolic_symbiosis, condition 3):
\[

\]
for a coupling constant $kappa_{text{symb}} > 0$ derived from the membrane stability
analysis (Thm. theorem:bk3_membrane_stability_criteria).

Sensitivity bound via Grönwall.
The metabolic rate $R_{text{meta}}$ (Def. definition:bk3_symbolic_metabolic_rate)

\[
r(t) leq frac{(1-kappa_{text{symb}})delta}{L_H}(e^{L_H t} - 1).

$C = (1-kappa_{text{symb}})(e^{L_H T}-1)$, giving
$|dR_{text{meta}}/ddelta| leq C < infty$ as required.

Since $C$ is finite and the operating band $[R_{text{min}}, R_{text{max}}]$
(Def. definition:bk3_symbolic_homeostasis) has positive width $geq 2Cdelta$
for sufficiently small $delta$, the perturbed metabolic rate remains within bounds.
Hence the system exhibits homeostatic reflexes (Thm. theorem:bk3_homeostatic_reflexes).

We prove each condition is necessary by contradiction.

Necessity of Condition 1 ($R_{text{meta}} in [R_{text{min}}, R_{text{max}}]$).
Suppose homeostasis fails: either $R_{text{meta}} < R_{text{min}}$ or
$R_{text{meta}} > R_{text{max}}$ persistently.
If $R_{text{meta}} < R_{text{min}}$, symbolic metabolism
(Def. definition:bk3_symbolic_metabolism) falls below the threshold
required to maintain membrane coherence; by
Thm. theorem:bk3_membrane_stability_criteria the free energy $F_i(beta_i)$
is no longer at a local minimum and restorative forces are lost, driving the
system toward collapse.
If $R_{text{meta}} > R_{text{max}}$, autophagic drift
(Def. definition:bk3_autophagic_drift) accelerates unboundedly; by the
H-theorem (Thm. theorem:bk2_h_theorem_for_symbolic_evol) entropy grows
monotonically and symbolic coherence is destroyed.
In either case persistent symbolic life is impossible.

Necessity of Condition 2 (Recurrent growth of $K(r)$).
Suppose $K(r)$ does not grow recurrently: there exists $R_0$ such that for all
$r_0 geq R_0$, $int_{r_0}^{r_0+T}(I'(s)-D'(s)) ds leq 0$ for every $T>0$.
By Thm. theorem:bk3_conditions_sustained_symbolic_growth, differentiation
dominates or balances integration, so $K(r)$ stagnates or fragments.
A stagnant $K(r)$ cannot adapt to perturbations in drift fields or coupling
parameters; under persistent drift (Def. definition:bk1_drift_field),
static symbolic structures lose coherence over time, and the system eventually
falls below the viability threshold, contradicting persistence.

Necessity of Condition 3 ($kappa_{text{symb}} > epsilon > 0$).
Suppose $kappa_{text{symb}} to 0$.
By Def. definition:bk3_symbiotic_curvature, this requires either
$S_i^{text{coupled}}/S_i^{text{isolated}} to 1$ (coupling ceases to enhance
stability) or $I(M_i;M_j) to 0$ (membranes become
informationally independent) for all pairs.

Def. definition:bk3_symbolic_symbiosis (condition 3) fails:
$\|delta D_i + delta D_i^{text{response}}\|_g to \|delta D_i\|_g$,

$kappa_{text{symb}}(A cup B) leq max(kappa_{text{symb}}(A),kappa_{text{symb}}(B))$,
each surviving independently — which is not persistent symbolic life in the
symbiotic sense required by the theorem statement.

Since the failure of any single condition destroys persistence, all three are necessary.

**Statement / Body**

The emergence of persistent symbolic life (Theorem theorem:bk3_criteria_persistent_symbolic_life), characterized by self-maintaining, self-modifying symbolic systems (Definition definition:bk3_symbolic_autopoiesis), naturally leads to the conditions necessary for symbolic evolution (cf. Def. definition:bk1_drift_field, Def. definition:bk1_reflection_operator, Def. definition:bk2_symbolic_free_energy, Def. definition:bk2_symbolic_entropy). Each such system remains bounded by the horizon of its own observer (cf. Def. definition:bk1_bounded_observer), so evolutionary pressure itself is refracted through the epistemic limits that Book IV will formalize. If we consider populations of such symbolic systems (or interacting membranes within a larger system), variations can arise through perturbations to drift fields (mutations) or changes in coupling. Differential stability and persistence (related to $S_i$, $kappa_{text{symb}}$, $K(r)$) provide a basis for selection, where more resilient or adaptive symbolic configurations are more likely to persist and influence future states. Coupling dynamics mediate interactions and competition/cooperation. Thus, the framework of symbolic thermodynamics and symbiosis potentially gives rise not merely to individual symbolic agents, but to entire ecosystems of evolving symbolic structures.
